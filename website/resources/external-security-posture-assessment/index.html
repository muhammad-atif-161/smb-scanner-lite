External assessment: the useful questions

What a public-domain assessment measures, how its findings are evidenced and why external visibility cannot establish complete security.

Online scan status

A defined outside view

An external security assessment collects observations available from outside a particular system. For a domain, these may include public records and services that deliberately respond to unauthenticated internet clients. The word external defines the point of view, not the size or quality of the security program.

State precisely what was examined

Before reading conclusions, identify the exact hostname, observation time, scanner profile, tools and exclusions. A DNS/email profile can examine sender policy and selected domain records. It cannot determine whether an employee's password was stolen or a business-critical backup can be restored.

Make each claim reproducible

For a conclusive finding, retain the observed value and the rule used to classify it. A transient DNS failure is inconclusive unless supported by further observations. A public interface responding to a request does not alone establish exploitability.

Keep the output proportional

Scores are summaries within the tested profile. They should not be repurposed as company-wide certifications. The important deliverable is a list of findings an owner can understand, investigate and verify after making changes.

Service scope: The prepared online scanner observes public DNS/email policy only. This reference may discuss controls that are not in that live profile.
