Product overview

An outside view, written so it can be acted on.

Most businesses cannot see their own public footprint the way a stranger does. SMB Scanner Lite looks from the outside, at a defined set of controls, and reports what it observed — without inventing certainty it does not have.

What a stranger can see

An outsider cannot read your policies, your firewall rules or your backups. They can see your DNS, your published mail policy, your TLS handshake, your response headers and whether certain services answer. That view is what this assessment measures.

Findings with evidence attached

Every conclusive finding quotes the record, header or handshake that produced it. There is no score without an observation behind it, and no observation is presented as an exploit.

A shared language for priority

The 0–100 score and A–F grade exist so an owner, an IT administrator and a consultant can agree on what to fix first — not so anyone can claim a business is secure.

Who it is for

  • SMB owners who need to know what is exposed before a customer asks
  • IT administrators and managed service providers who need a defensible starting point
  • Cybersecurity consultants who want evidence, not a sales deck
  • Defensive practitioners learning how external assessment actually works

What it deliberately is not

  • Not a vulnerability scanner: nothing is exploited or fuzzed
  • Not a penetration test: it cannot prove a weakness is exploitable
  • Not a certification: a high score is not a statement that you are secure
  • Not unrestricted: the public edition cannot be pointed at arbitrary hosts

Why an owner should care

Technical checks mapped to business consequences.

The report keeps the technical evidence, but the first question is practical: what could this mean for your email, your access, your customers or your next security questionnaire?

For non-technical owners. You do not need to know DNS, TLS or security headers to use this. The checks answer four practical questions most small-business owners actually care about.

Fraudulent email that looks like it came from you

Email authentication checks show whether a stranger can send invoices, password-reset messages or customer emails in your name.

Remote access or file shares left open to the internet

Network exposure checks look for high-risk services such as SMB file sharing and Remote Desktop that answer from the public internet.

Failing a customer or vendor security questionnaire

The assessment produces a clear score, grade and evidence you can attach when a customer asks how you protect your public-facing systems.

An embarrassing public security surprise

DNS, TLS and header checks surface common configuration gaps before they become public incidents or lost deals.

  • Measured onlyA control that timed out is excluded from the score and reported as inconclusive, so silence can neither inflate nor deflate the result.
  • Weighted by consequenceEmail authentication carries the largest weight (25%), followed by DNS, transport and exposure (20% each) and browser headers (15%).
  • Grade bands are fixedA 90–100, B 80–89, C 65–79, D 50–64, F below 50. The same bands apply to every target.
  • Not a certificateThe score says nothing about your applications, your people, your processes or your physical security.

How it works

Four steps, one of which is authorisation.

The assessment is read-only end to end. What changes between a public demonstration and a private assessment is the target, the scope and the authorisation behind it.

01

Select an authorised target

An assessment needs a domain you own or are permitted to assess. The public edition is limited to five reserved testing domains; a private assessment starts with written authorisation.

02

Run the read-only checks

DNS, mail authentication records, TLS handshakes, HTTP headers and protocol-level service probes. No login, no payload, no exploitation.

03

Collect the evidence

Each check stores what it observed and the status it reached — pass, warning, fail or inconclusive. A timeout is never converted into a failure.

04

Review score and report

A weighted 0–100 score, an A–F grade, findings ordered by severity, and a report you can read as HTML, JSON, CSV or TXT.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

Short answers, no upsell. The methodology page has the longer technical version of most of these.

What is SMB Scanner Lite?

SMB Scanner Lite is an evidence-first external security posture assessment for small and medium businesses. It evaluates 22 publicly observable security controls on a domain, produces a 0–100 posture score and an A–F grade, and attaches the observation behind every conclusive finding.

What does the 0–100 score mean?

It is a weighted summary of the controls that completed: pass earns full credit, warning partial credit, fail no credit, and inconclusive results are excluded entirely. It exists so an owner and their IT helper can agree on priority. It is a communication aid, not a certificate.

Can I scan my own domain on this website?

Not on this public site. The published edition is hard-locked to five free-to-use testing domains - example.com, example.org, example.net, scanme.nmap.org and badssl.com - and every other name is rejected before a network request is made. To assess a domain you own, request an assessment or run the sandbox locally.

Is the demo a live scan?

The demo on this site is a recorded assessment: stored output from a genuine run against example.com on 28 October 2026, with the timestamp, report ID, scanner version and consent receipt shown alongside it. Nothing you enter triggers a scan from your browser.

Can it detect a breach or tell me if I have been hacked?

No. External observation cannot establish compromise. It can show the exposure that often precedes one - missing mail authentication, an open remote-access service, weak transport configuration - and that is the honest limit of what it claims.
