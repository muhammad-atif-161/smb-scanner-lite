Methodology

Built around evidence, not unexplained scores.

The rules behind the checks, the scoring and the limitations — in the order a reviewer would ask about them.

The rules

Read the methodology before you trust the number.

What is actually measured

Only what a stranger on the public internet can observe without authenticating: DNS records, mail authentication policy, TLS handshakes, HTTP response headers, and whether a small set of high-risk services answers a protocol-level probe. Nothing behind a login, nothing on a private network, nothing that requires a credential.

Evidence and the four statuses

Every check ends in one of four states. The distinction matters more than the colour.

  • PassThe observed control meets the published expectation.
  • WarningThe control exists but is weaker than intended, or partially configured.
  • FailThe control is absent, or what was observed contradicts the expectation.
  • InconclusiveNo conclusive answer arrived before the timeout. This is not a failure.
Scoring and grading

Each completed control contributes to a 0–100 posture score, weighted by how much it matters to a small business. The score covers only controls that finished.

  • Measured onlyA timed-out control is excluded from the score and reported separately as inconclusive.
  • Grade bandsA–B strong, C fair, D needs work, F urgent.
  • A communication aidThe score helps an owner and an IT helper agree on priority. It is not a guarantee, and it is not a penetration-test certificate.
A failed check is not a confirmed vulnerability

A failed control means the published expectation was not met. It is not an exploit, and it is not evidence that a system has been compromised. Treat every finding as a reason to look — then verify it yourself.

Limitations and false positives

External observation is inherently limited. A control can be configured correctly and still look wrong from one network, one resolver, or one point in time.

  • Location and resolver dependenceA record that resolves differently for you may be correct for the domain owner.
  • TimeoutsSilence is not a verdict. The tool refuses to convert it into a failure.
  • No exploitationNothing is logged in to, fuzzed, or exploited, so a check cannot confirm whether a weakness is actually exploitable.
  • ScopeThe score says nothing about applications, people, processes or physical security.
How to verify a finding yourself

Each conclusive result quotes the record, header or handshake that produced it. Copy it, then run the equivalent query from a machine outside your network and compare.

If the answer differs, the finding is stale or specific to the vantage point used. That is a normal result, not a fault in the tool.

Reporting

Every report carries its own proof of authorisation.

Before any scan runs, the operator accepts the privacy policy and signs a consent receipt. That receipt is embedded in the report, hash-chained to the previous one, and stored on the machine that ran the scan.

  • consent_id, timestamp, operator and target
  • policy version and its SHA-256 digest
  • previous receipt hash and the receipt hash
  • Stored locally — never uploaded to this website
