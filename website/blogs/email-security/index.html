Blog · Email security

Email security, from the records up

Email is where most small businesses are impersonated first, because forging a sender is cheap and the invoice that follows looks exactly like a real one. These guides are the fixes, in the order that does not break your mail.

3 posts Updated 31 October 2026 Cluster guide

There are three separate mechanisms in play, and they are routinely confused with each other. SPF describes which servers may send mail for your domain. DKIM signs the message so a receiver can tell whether it was altered in transit. DMARC is the policy that decides what to do when either check fails — and, critically, whether the domain the receiver checks is the domain your customer actually sees in their inbox.

It is common to find SPF published, less common to find a working DKIM signature on every sender, and rarer still to find a DMARC policy that enforces anything — because enforcement is the step that makes missing mail somebody's problem. The guides here take that sequence seriously: inventory first, reporting second, enforcement last.

Every post in this cluster ends with the same question: what would you do on Monday? Where a check can be run by hand, the command is in the post. Where it cannot, the post says so rather than implying a tool will handle it.
