When legitimate business email lands in spam

A message can authenticate perfectly and still be classified as unwanted. That is a different failure.

Muhammad Atif Updated 4 November 2026 2 min read

Worth remembering

  • Read the received message's authentication headers before changing DNS.
  • Authentication and deliverability are related but not identical.
  • Avoid changing multiple records at once without testing.

A customer says your quote landed in spam. The first response is often to edit SPF or add another DKIM record, but that can make diagnosis harder. Authentication establishes who sent a message and whether it was altered. Inbox placement also responds to reputation, recipient behavior and local provider policy.

Get a sample that actually failed

Ask for the original message headers or reproduce the issue in a mailbox you control. Look for the sending IP, authentication outcomes and the provider's rejection or filtering reason. If the message never arrived, investigate bounce notices and SMTP response codes before guessing at spam-folder behavior.

Separate alignment from simple passes

An SPF pass for a marketing vendor's envelope domain does not automatically establish DMARC alignment with your visible From domain. A DKIM pass for an unrelated signing domain can be similarly insufficient. Read the complete authentication results, including the domains involved.

Then examine sending quality

Confirm that the sending domain has coherent forward and reverse DNS where appropriate, that the message platform is properly configured, and that the content does not mimic phishing techniques. Unusual bursts, stale lists and high complaint rates can affect reputation even with excellent DNS policy.

Change one variable, then test

Document the problem, make a targeted change and test mail through the affected system. Ask the recipient provider for feedback when possible. A public DNS assessment helps rule in certain configuration issues, but it does not read an organization's mail traffic or promise inbox placement.

The service boundary: The prepared online assessment verifies domain ownership and inspects public DNS/email policy. This article may discuss broader security practices; it is not a promise that the hosted worker performs those checks. See the actual scan scope.
