How to read a security report without being misled

The first page gives you a score. The rest should give you the reasons, boundaries and a plan.

Muhammad Atif Updated 4 November 2026 2 min read

Worth remembering

  • Read scope and timestamp before reacting to a grade.
  • Treat inconclusive as unknown, not as a pass or vulnerability.
  • Assign a remediation owner and verify the exact evidence afterward.

A security report is easy to skim badly: see a grade, search for the red labels and forward the PDF to IT. That misses the most useful information. The score is a summary of selected observations, not an answer to whether the business is generally secure.

Find the boundary first

Identify the exact domain, timestamp and tested profile. For SMB Scanner Lite's initial online service, that profile is DNS/email authentication. Do not assume TLS, application testing or internet-exposed ports were evaluated.

Match each finding to its observation

Look for the DNS value, response and criterion that produced the status. If a report says DMARC requests monitoring only, read the actual p=none policy and consider whether that matches the business's rollout plan. If the tool did not receive a reliable answer, it should not claim the record is missing.

Handle inconclusive results separately

Unknown outcomes need investigation. A timeout could reflect provider behavior, network conditions or a temporary outage. It should not enter a remediation queue as a definite vulnerability without more evidence.

Turn observations into accountable work

For each issue, record an owner, intended change and verification step. Test legitimate email flows before tightening DMARC. After changes propagate, repeat the observation from a comparable vantage and keep the original result for comparison.

Don't buy certainty the method cannot offer

Even an excellent score within a narrow scan profile says nothing about passwords, internal systems, backups or the safety of an application. Use the report to direct attention, not to replace a broader risk program.

The service boundary: The prepared online assessment verifies domain ownership and inspects public DNS/email policy. This article may discuss broader security practices; it is not a promise that the hosted worker performs those checks. See the actual scan scope.
