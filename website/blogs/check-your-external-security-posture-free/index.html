A free first look at your domain's public records

Before you run any scanner, you can learn a useful amount from the records your domain already publishes.

Muhammad Atif Updated 4 November 2026 2 min read

Worth remembering

  • Start with your domain's public DNS responses and current provider list.
  • Interpret SPF/DMARC records as policy, not evidence of actual mail delivery.
  • Never expand testing beyond systems you control.

Domain security can feel abstract because the relevant configuration is usually hidden inside a hosting dashboard. Yet the DNS records themselves are public. With a domain you control and a few carefully chosen queries, you can begin an inventory without installing a security product.

Identify the responsible providers

Make a note of the registrar, authoritative name servers, email provider and website host. Check the answers against what the business believes it owns. Unexpected records may be deliberate delegation or stale configuration; investigate before removing them.

Read the published mail policy

Use a standard DNS lookup tool to inspect TXT records at the domain apex and at _dmarc. Look for duplicate SPF records, contradictory policy and whether DMARC requests monitoring or stronger treatment. A policy that exists is not necessarily implemented correctly for outgoing messages.

Ask whether the evidence supports the conclusion

DNS responses can be cached and may differ by resolver. If you cannot retrieve a record, verify at the authoritative source before declaring it absent. DKIM selectors are not universally discoverable, so do not claim a signing failure from a short selector list.

Keep a simple baseline

Save the values, provider names and time of inspection. After changing any record, repeat the same queries. The value lies in noticing differences you intended—and differences you did not.
