A careful path from DMARC monitoring to enforcement

Rejecting mail is easy to write in DNS. Knowing what you would reject takes considerably more care.

Muhammad Atif Updated 4 November 2026 2 min read

Worth remembering

  • Inventory all legitimate senders before enforcement.
  • Look for aligned SPF or DKIM on messages, not only published records.
  • Move policy deliberately and monitor legitimate delivery.

The temptation is to copy a strong-looking DMARC record into DNS and call email security finished. That is risky when invoices, payroll messages or customer confirmations come from services nobody has inventoried. Enforcement can expose a configuration problem by rejecting the messages your business relies on.

Start with the sending map

Write down every system that sends using your company domain: primary mailbox provider, website forms, CRM, help desk, marketing provider and third-party billing software. Record who owns each integration. A forgotten vendor is often more significant than a missing DNS tag.

Observe before enforcing

Publish a syntactically valid DMARC policy in monitoring mode with a reporting destination you control or a trusted processor. Analyze aggregate reports for legitimate sources and unexpected senders. Reports may contain sensitive operational detail, so treat them as business records rather than unrestricted analytics data.

Send actual messages from each known system to a test mailbox and inspect Authentication-Results. Look for a passing, aligned authentication method. A DMARC policy cannot fix an improperly configured outgoing service by itself.

Fix sender configuration one provider at a time

For each authorized sender, confirm DKIM signing and/or aligned SPF behavior. Avoid adding SPF includes blindly: a single record may have lookup limits, and broad authorization increases attack surface. Verify that delegated domains and subdomains follow the intended policy.

Advance in measured stages

When legitimate mail consistently authenticates, evaluate moving toward quarantine and then reject. The precise schedule depends on the organization. Keep a rollback procedure, monitor delivery failures, and coordinate with the people who receive billing and support complaints before tightening enforcement.

A DNS-only scan can show the published DMARC state. It cannot know which of your providers will be disrupted. The operational rollout remains your responsibility.
